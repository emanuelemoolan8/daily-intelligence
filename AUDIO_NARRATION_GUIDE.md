# Daily Intelligence — conversational audio standard

Write the spoken edition as a friendly, knowledgeable person explaining news to another person. This applies to English and Malayalam.

- Lead with what happened, explain why it happened, and explain why it matters to listeners.
- Use short, spoken sentences, natural transitions and pauses. Avoid stiff headline reading and repeated filler.
- Malayalam must use everyday, easy-to-understand language. English should be equally simple and engaging.
- Do not change or invent dates, figures, quotations, attributions or uncertainty. Keep opinion separate from reporting.
- The brief edition should be concise but meaningful. The full edition should add context and explanations without needless repetition.
- Prepare spoken scripts from verified published facts **before** generating audio; written articles and spoken scripts may differ in style.
- Generate audio from those final spoken scripts and check that the playback, language, duration and narration match.
- Current generation script adds friendly openings and transitions around published text. It does not yet conversationally rewrite the article content itself; that is a separate editorial step.
- Free Edge neural voices can sound natural, but cannot guarantee the expression, pacing or emotional range of a real human narrator.
