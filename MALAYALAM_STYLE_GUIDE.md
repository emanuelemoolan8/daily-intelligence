# Daily Intelligence — Malayalam writing standard

Use simple, natural Malayalam for every new edition, including titles, summaries, detailed explanations, weather labels and narration scripts.

- Write as if explaining the news to a friend in Kerala. Avoid stiff newspaper Malayalam and literal translations from English.
- Prefer short sentences (one idea at a time) and common everyday words. Keep the facts, figures, names, sources, and important uncertainty intact.
- Explain unfamiliar business, science, politics and technology terms in plain Malayalam on first use. Retain the English term in parentheses only when it helps understanding.
- Do not shorten the reporting merely to simplify it. Keep useful context, causes, consequences and what remains unknown.
- Use natural spoken phrasing suitable for listening. Read each paragraph aloud mentally and rewrite any sentence that sounds awkward.
- Never invent or overstate facts when simplifying.
- Malayalam UI labels, status messages and notifications should follow the same standard.
- Generate Malayalam MP3 narration from the final simplified Malayalam script, not an older version.

Examples:
- "മൂലധനച്ചെലവ് കൂടുന്നു" → "പണം കണ്ടെത്താൻ കൂടുതൽ ചെലവാകുന്നു"
- "അടിസ്ഥാനസൗകര്യ നിക്ഷേപം" → "റോഡുകൾ, വൈദ്യുതി, കെട്ടിടങ്ങൾ തുടങ്ങിയ സൗകര്യങ്ങൾക്കായി ചെലവിടുന്ന പണം" (adjust to context)
- "ഉപയോഗനിരക്ക് ഉയർന്നു" → "കൂടുതൽ ആളുകൾ ഇത് ഉപയോഗിക്കുന്നു" (only if that is what the metric measures)
- "ഔദ്യോഗിക സ്ഥിരീകരണം ലഭിച്ചിട്ടില്ല" → "ഇത് ശരിയാണെന്ന് അധികൃതർ ഇതുവരെ പറഞ്ഞിട്ടില്ല"

Editorial checklist before publishing: Is it easy to understand on first listen? Are technical terms explained? Are sentences short? Are facts and uncertainty preserved? Does the generated audio match the published text?
